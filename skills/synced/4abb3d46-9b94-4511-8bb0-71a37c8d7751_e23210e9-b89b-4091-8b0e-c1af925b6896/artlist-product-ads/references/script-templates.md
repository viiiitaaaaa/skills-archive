# Script Templates

## The 7-shot structure

Works for any physical product with a craft, material, or convenience story. 4–5 seconds per shot, 28–33 seconds total. Drop shots 5 or 6 to reach a tighter 24 seconds.

| # | Function | What's in frame | Camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | Product recognizable, arresting light — held to backlight, in motion | Slight turn toward light, minimal push-in |
| 2 | Proof of material | Extreme macro, texture and finish visible | Slow push-in along the surface |
| 3 | Origin story | Hands making, assembling, finishing it | Near-still, slight push-in |
| 4 | On the body / in place | Product worn or in its context, elegant motion | Near-still |
| 5 | The convenience beat | The one physical action that removes friction | Follows the action |
| 6 | Lifestyle proof | Product during a real moment of the buyer's life | Near-still |
| 7 | Close | Product at rest, hero composition, room for the slogan | Slow pull-back |

The order is deliberate. Desire before explanation, proof before lifestyle, slogan last. Shot 5 exists because most e-commerce objections are physical, not aesthetic.

Users often prune this to four or five shots, and the ones they cut are usually 3 and 5 — the origin story and the convenience beat — because they're the hardest to picture. Those are also the two that do the selling. When a shot goes, move its argument into a subline or a voice line rather than letting it vanish, and say that you've done so.

## Hook patterns for shot 1

Ranked by how reliably they stop a scroll:

**Negation of the category.** "This isn't a watch band. It's jewelry." Reframes the product into a higher-value category in four words. Strongest general-purpose opener.

**Provocation.** "Your [category] is boring." Higher stopping power, but the tone is aggressive and can misfit calm or premium brands.

**The unexpected fact.** "No two are alike." Works when the material genuinely varies — natural stone, wood grain, hand-dyed textile.

**Direct question.** "How is this made?" Weakest for cold traffic, decent for retargeting.

## Caption vs voice-over

Captions and voice-over should not be identical. The caption is the headline the muted viewer reads; the voice-over expands it. Most short-form viewing starts muted, so the captions alone must carry the full sales argument.

Captions: 2–5 words, all caps or title case, one idea.
Voice-over: 9–12 words per 4–5 second shot, conversational, expands the caption with a reason.

If the finished cut uses two-tier captions — and it usually should — each shot also needs a 2–4 word subline. Write these in the script table, not at assembly time. The subline is where an argument goes when no shot demonstrates it: "no clasp, no fuss" over a restaurant scene still closes the objection that the ad never shows the band being put on.

Add a closing brand tag for the end card: three words, one line ("It's <brand>"), roughly 1.2 seconds.

Worked example:

| # | Caption | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Not a watch band. | This isn't a watch band. It's jewelry. |
| 2 | Real African turquoise. | Real African turquoise. No two stones are ever alike. |
| 3 | Strung by hand. | Strung by hand, one bead at a time. Never mass produced. |
| 4 | One bead at a time. | — |
| 5 | Slips on in one second. | Stretch it, slide it on. One second, and you're ready. |
| 6 | No clasps. No buckles. | Nothing to fasten. Nothing to fumble with. It just fits. |
| 7 | Ancient Stones. Modern Soul. | Ancient stones. Modern soul. Wear something with a story. |

## Timing arithmetic

English runs about 2.2 words per second at a calm advertising pace; other languages differ, and the same script in Russian runs roughly 10% longer. Budget 9–11 words per shot and verify against the `durationSeconds` the voice-over tool returns.

If a line overruns its clip, prefer cutting words to lengthening the clip — clip length costs credits, words cost nothing. Slowing a clip 10–15% during assembly is invisible on slow camera moves and free.

## Claims discipline

Only claim what the product does. "Comfortable during workouts" on an elastic stone bracelet invites returns and disputes — "comfortable during yoga and meditation" is true, and lands with the same buyer.

Avoid medical, healing, or energy claims as statements of fact. Atmosphere and imagery carry that association without the liability: show meditation, morning light, calm. Let the buyer draw the conclusion.
